Method & boundaries

A forecast should show its working.

Calibra is a design concept for a La Liga forecast that treats uncertainty as part of the product, not a footnote. It is deliberately calm, legible and honest about its edges.

Design prototype · no live data · no backend

Built as an isolated frontend experiment. The existing predictor and its APIs are not connected to this site.

Premise

Predictions are more useful when they show their edges.

Most football forecasts compress the match into a single pick. Calibra starts from the opposite habit: keep the full scoreline distribution visible, then explain the shape. The favourite is a summary, not the whole argument.

The concept combines a low-parameter goals model with a context model, blends them at a fixed weight and calibrates the result. The closing market stays in the room as a benchmark — not an opponent to beat at all costs.

This site is a designed argument, not a live product. The numbers are plausible and internally consistent, but they are fixed for the prototype. The existing predictor is not called, copied or changed.

01 / GOALS

Start with a scoreline model.

A Dixon–Coles baseline uses each side’s scoring and conceding rates, venue and time decay. It is quiet, transparent and produces every scoreline, not just a winner.

02 / CONTEXT

Add the context a goals model misses.

A boosted model reads form, rest, venue splits and rating asymmetries. It is sharper on the edges of a fixture and more willing to disagree with the baseline.

03 / BLEND

Blend, then calibrate.

A fixed 60/40 blend keeps the scoreline shape coherent. Calibration then trims overconfidence, especially in the middle bands where draws live.

04 / PUBLISH

Publish the whole record.

RPS, log loss, accuracy, calibration and the weak spots sit on the same shelf. A forecast that cannot be checked is just a confident sentence.

01

Distribution first.

The scoreline is the unit of the forecast. Headline probabilities are a compact view of a larger field.

02

Show the misses.

Performance is a record, not a highlight reel. The market gap and the failure modes stay visible.

03

Keep it legible.

Typography, contrast and motion are part of the argument. A forecast nobody can read is not honest.

Boundaries

What this prototype cannot do.

The concept is deliberately honest about its edges. These limits are part of the design, not a disclaimer bolted onto it.

  • ×No live data. Kickoff times, results and probabilities are hardcoded for the prototype. Nothing is fetched.
  • ×No team news. Late injuries, suspensions and rotation are invisible. A pre-match forecast cannot see a late change.
  • ×No betting advice. The market is a benchmark, not a target. This is not a gambling tool and does not recommend stakes.
  • ×No production model. The scoreline grid is a local Poisson illustration. The project’s real model is not connected.

What is illustrative?

Every fixture, probability, chart and performance number on this site is mock data created for the design concept. Nothing is fetched from the existing predictor, its database, its APIs or its model outputs.

The match detail pages use a simple Poisson grid to make the scoreline visualisation coherent within the prototype. That is a local illustration, not the project’s model. The four model descriptions are also illustrative.

The design is intentionally separate from the production app. No existing pages, components, global styles, layouts, data files or functionality were modified to build it.

Craft notes.

Keyboard navigation, visible focus rings, 44px minimum interactive targets and a / shortcut for fixture search. The scoreline grid supports arrow-key inspection.

The site honours prefers-reduced-motion, prefers-reduced-transparency and prefers-contrast: more. Colour is never the only signal: outcomes carry labels and values, and form chips carry letters.

Two local typefaces carry the identity: Instrument Sans for the functional layer, Instrument Serif for the editorial layer. No web font request leaves the page.
