How it works

How it works. No black box.

Every La Liga fixture on this site gets a predicted scoreline, not just a winner. Two models produce it: one from goals scored and conceded, one from the situation around the match. Here is what each does, and what neither can see.

      Limitations

      What it cannot do.

      A prediction is only useful if you know where it stops.

      ×

      No team news.

      Late injuries, suspensions and rotation are invisible. The forecast is made from what happened before the team sheets.

      ×

      Pre-match only.

      A scoreline forecast is locked before kick-off. Nothing is re-priced while a match is in play.

      ×

      Behind the market.

      The bookmaker's closing prices still score better. Calibra is close, and says so on the performance page.

      ×

      Not betting advice.

      Probabilities describe what tends to happen across many similar matches. They are not tips, and no stake is ever suggested.

      What a percentage actually means here

      A 65% home-win figure is not a promise that the home side wins. It means that across many matches like this one, the home side would win about 65 times in 100. A single result can always contradict it, and often will.

      That is why the record reports calibration alongside accuracy: accuracy throws away the confidence attached to each call, calibration does not.

      How the scoreline grid is built

      Each side gets an expected-goals figure. Two Poisson distributions — one per side — are multiplied across every scoreline from 0–0 to 6–6, then normalised so the 225 cells add up to 100%.

      The grid is shown only when the pipeline exported one for that match. No inference runs in the browser.

      Why 65/35 and not a tuned weight

      A fixed weight is a decision, not a claim of optimality. Tuning it on the same matches used to report performance would make the record easier to like and harder to trust.

      The goals model is the stable base; the context model corrects for form, rest and venue asymmetries. The split reflects that hierarchy.
